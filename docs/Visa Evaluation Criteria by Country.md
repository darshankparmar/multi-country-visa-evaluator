# Visa Evaluation Criteria by Country

## Ireland

### Critical Skills Employment Permit

The **Critical Skills Employment Permit** is designed to attract highly skilled workers in shortage occupations. Key criteria include: a valid job offer in an eligible occupation (often on a published shortage list) for at least 2 years, plus **salary and qualifications**. The salary must meet a high threshold: generally **≥ €38,000** per year for “critical” roles (with a relevant degree or equivalent experience), or **≥ €64,000** for other eligible occupations[\[1\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,national%20who%20does%20not%20have). The applicant must possess relevant tertiary qualifications or commensurate experience, matching the job requirements[\[1\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,national%20who%20does%20not%20have). No Labour Market Test is required, and employers can be **waived** from the 50:50 EEA workforce rule through support by enterprise agencies[\[2\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,Ireland%20or%20IDA%20Ireland%20only). Crucially, **family reunification is allowed** (spouses and children can accompany and obtain multi-year permits), and after 2 years the permit holder can apply directly for long-term residency[\[3\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,currently%20issued%20free%20of%20charge)[\[1\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,national%20who%20does%20not%20have).

*Required documents* include the standard employment permit application, the signed employment contract, proof of the employee’s identity (passport), qualifications (degree certificates, CV) and evidence of the employer’s bona fides. (A sample checklist is provided on the [Irish government site](https://enterprise.gov.ie)[\[4\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=The%20main%20criteria%20applied%20in,these%20cases%20are)[\[5\]](https://us.iasservices.org.uk/ie/work/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=,telephone%20number%20and%20email%20address).) The **employer** must be registered in Ireland, and the application typically comes from the employer or an agent. There is **no point-based system**; instead the decision is rule-based (degree \+ salary \+ occupation). The **salary threshold and occupation match matter most**, because they are legal minimums. Unique rules: after 2 years on this permit, holders can stay on a Stamp 4 permission and apply for family permits; also some occupations are explicitly excluded (Ineligible list).

### General Employment Permit

The **General Employment Permit** covers a broad range of jobs not on the ineligible list. Evaluation focuses on the **job contract, salary, and qualifications**. The **minimum salary** is lower (at least **€30,000**, effectively €34,000 including standard allowances)[\[4\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=The%20main%20criteria%20applied%20in,these%20cases%20are). The position must not be on the “excluded” list, and the applicant must have the **skills/qualifications or experience** required for the role[\[4\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=The%20main%20criteria%20applied%20in,these%20cases%20are). Crucially, the employer must conduct a full **Labour Market Needs Test** (advertising the vacancy in national/local media and registering with the employment office) to show no suitable EEA worker is available[\[6\]](https://us.iasservices.org.uk/ie/work/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=,other%20advertisements%20for%20the%20role). Employers can also rely on the 50:50 rule (no more than 50% of staff from non-EEA countries), or obtain a waiver via Enterprise Ireland. Unlike Critical Skills, **spouses and dependents do not get automatic work rights**[\[7\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=Spouses%2C%20dependants%20or%20partners%20of,permit%20in%20their%20own%20right).

Documents include the permit application form, contract, detailed job description, qualifications, passport copies and the **advertising evidence** for the labour market test[\[8\]](https://us.iasservices.org.uk/ie/work/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=,other%20advertisements%20for%20the%20role). Employers must have a valid Employer Registration Number and prove corporate status. If the applicant is in Ireland, proof of immigration status (GNIB card) is needed. There is **no formal points system**; the main **rules** are salary above the minimum and completion of the labour test. The **highest weight** is given to meeting the salary and qualification requirements, since failure on those grounds alone triggers refusal[\[4\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=The%20main%20criteria%20applied%20in,these%20cases%20are). Unique rules: first-time permit holders must stay 9 months with the initial employer (unless exceptions), and after 5 years on this permit one can apply for Stamp 4 (long-term residency)[\[9\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=If%20the%20foreign%20national%20is,hand%2C%20not%20unduly%20binding%20the)[\[10\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=Holders%20of%C2%A0%20General%20Employment%20Permits,for%205%20years%20or%20more).

## Poland

Poland issues several **typed work permits** depending on the job and employer: **Type A, B, C, E** (and others not requested here).

* **Type A (Standard Employment):** For a contract of employment with a Polish employer. Key criteria: a valid employment contract in Poland, and a **negative labour market statement** from the local employment office (“starosta”) confirming no suitable Polish/EEA worker is available[\[11\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-a/#:~:text=5,fulfillment%20of%20qualification%20requirements%20and). No fixed salary floor is set, but the contract must meet Polish payroll and social security norms. Required documents include the work permit application, the foreigner’s passport, the employer’s company documents (registration forms), the starosta’s statement, and proof of the foreigner’s qualifications or experience[\[12\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-a/#:~:text=1,travel%20document%20of%20the%20foreigner)[\[13\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-a/#:~:text=6,application%20for%20a%C2%A0work%20permit%20for). The employer essentially “sponsors” the permit. Type A permits do not use a point system; approval hinges on providing all documents and satisfying the labour test. The labour market statement and contract terms (hours, duties) are the **critical factors**. Unique: there is no explicit salary threshold, but all payments are subject to the Polish social security system.

* **Type B (Manager/Executive):** For a member of the management board or general partner in a Polish company. Criteria: the foreigner must hold a corporate position (e.g. board member) in the employing entity and be in Poland over 6 months per year[\[14\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-b/#:~:text=It%20refers%20to%20a%C2%A0situation%20where,in%20the%20following%2012%20months). Documents include the employment application, company formation deeds, proof of the employer’s income (previous year financials) and employment numbers[\[15\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-b/#:~:text=1,travel%20document%20of%20the%20foreigner)[\[16\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-b/#:~:text=5,statement%20of%20no%20criminal%20conviction). No labour test is needed. The job itself (corporate governance role) is the key criterion. There is no salary requirement per se, but the company’s financial statements must show the means to pay and employment levels. Unique: aimed at executives; if the company’s tax or employment details are deficient, the application may be refused.

* **Type C (Intra-Company Transfer):** For employees of a foreign company posted to its branch/affiliate in Poland for \>30 days in a year. The foreign employer and the Polish entity must have a relationship (same president or common assets)[\[17\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-c/#:~:text=3,of%20a%C2%A0foreign%20and%20domestic%20company). Required documents include proof of the foreign company’s legal status, the relationship between foreign and Polish entities, evidence the person is employed abroad (foreign contract), passport, and a statement identifying a local representative[\[18\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-c/#:~:text=1,of%20the%20Republic%20of%20Poland)[\[19\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-c/#:~:text=6,territory%20of%20Republic%20of%20Poland). There is no fixed wage rule; the transfer itself and corporate link are the focus. Critical factors: the genuine inter-company link and the assignment purpose. Unique: this is essentially a corporate secondment permit (similar in spirit to EU ICT), and it does not require a labour market test.

* **Type E (Contractor/ITC2):** For foreign employees of a foreign company sent to Poland for specific projects (\<6 months in 6 months, not covered by Type C). Key criteria: a formal contract between the foreign employer and a Polish company (often a project agreement)[\[20\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-e/#:~:text=6,and%20the%20posting%20of%20workers), plus a declaration by the Polish employer confirming the assignment terms[\[21\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-e/#:~:text=7,period%20of%20entrustment%20of%20work). Documents include the permit application, foreign company status, the employment contract, the inter-company cooperation agreement, Polish employer’s declaration (with details: role, hours, pay)[\[20\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-e/#:~:text=6,and%20the%20posting%20of%20workers)[\[22\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-e/#:~:text=8,the%20fee). There is no salary threshold, but all work terms (especially salary) must comply with Polish labor law. The **existence of an assignment contract and bilateral agreement** are the defining factors. Unique: this permit bridges foreign-outsourcing work in Poland; it requires both companies to coordinate and demonstrate the transfer terms.

## France

### Talent Passport (“Passeport Talent”)

France’s **Talent Passport** is a multi-year permit for various high-skill categories. For a **skilled employee (salarié qualifié)**, criteria include: a job contract in France \>3 months, typically linked to R\&D or innovation, with **salary ≥ 2× minimum wage**[\[23\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=,time%20worker). The employee must hold at least a Master’s (or 5 years’ experience) if a degree is required by law[\[24\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=,experience%20at%20a%20comparable%20level). For **specialist postings (“salarié en mission”)** within a corporate group, requirements are: at least 3 months’ seniority with the group abroad, a French contract \>3 months, and **salary ≥ 1.8× minimum wage**[\[25\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,SMIC). Documents include the Talent Passport visa application, forms completed by the employer (e.g. form *15614/15616*), the signed employment contract, company documentation (proof of innovative status or group affiliation), and proof of salary and qualifications[\[26\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=In%20addition%20to%20any%20supporting,tax%20provisions%20favouring%20innovative%20companies)[\[25\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,SMIC). No points system is used; decisions follow fixed rules. **Salary multiplier and contract length** are the most important criteria (if not met, refusal is automatic). Unique rules: holders get a 4-year renewable residence permit, and family members (spouses, children) receive work rights[\[27\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=Your%20family%20members%20may%20also,your%20local%20police%20station%20in). The Talent Passport exempt foreign employees from France’s labor test (no requirement to advertise the job), and it grants a path to permanent residency after 3–4 years.

### Salarié en Mission (Employee on Assignment)

This is a sub-type of the Talent Passport for **intracompany transferees** (often called “corporate secondment”). Key conditions: the applicant must be transferred within a company group. If employed by a French entity of a multinational, a contract \>3 months with the French company and proof the stay is an intracompany transfer are needed[\[25\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,SMIC). The gross salary must be at least **1.8× the French minimum wage** (SMIC)[\[25\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,SMIC). Alternatively, if formally seconded to an affiliated French company, the candidate needs at least 3 months’ seniority in the group, a management or expert role, evidence of repatriation plan after the mission, and **a salary appropriate to the post**[\[28\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,of%20the%20mission%20in%20France). Required documents include the transfer contract, assignment letter, French work contract, proof of group relationship, and salary proof. There is **no labor market test**[\[25\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=,SMIC). The emphasis is on the intracompany relationship and mission purpose; salary threshold is a key numeric criterion. Unique aspects: the permit is valid up to 4 years and renewable if the assignment continues[\[29\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/highly-qualified-worker-france_en#:~:text=The%20%E2%80%9Cpassport%20talent%E2%80%9D%20residence%20permit,your%20assignment%20in%20France%20continues), and applications for family reunification are processed at the same time as the visa (spouses need no French language)[\[30\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Family%20reunification%20is%20based%20on,also%20be%20made%20in%20Germany).

## Netherlands

### Knowledge Migrant Permit

The Netherlands’ **Highly Skilled Migrant (Kennismigrant)** permit requires a *recognized sponsor* employer[\[31\]](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant#:~:text=,And%20do%20you%20practise%20a) and a **salary above a set threshold**. For 2025, the gross monthly salary must be at least **€5,688** if the worker is 30 or older (for “standard” highly skilled migrants)[\[32\]](https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements#application-to-work-as-a-highly-skilled-migrant-and-for-the-european-blue-card#:~:text=Highly%20skilled%20migrants%20younger%20than,00)[\[33\]](https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements#application-to-work-as-a-highly-skilled-migrant-and-for-the-european-blue-card#:~:text=,00); if under 30 years old (or a recent graduate), the threshold is €4,171. There is also a **reduced threshold of €2,989** for recent graduates (orientation year graduates)[\[34\]](https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements#application-to-work-as-a-highly-skilled-migrant-and-for-the-european-blue-card#:~:text=Highly%20skilled%20migrants%20reduced%20salary,00). The worker must have an employment contract with the sponsor and appropriate qualifications (usually a bachelor’s degree or higher)[\[31\]](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant#:~:text=,And%20do%20you%20practise%20a). Required documents: employer’s permit application (Form IWV001), contract, salary evidence, the migrant’s passport, degree certificates and possibly a degree evaluation. No labor test is needed. Criteria are deterministic: sponsor status and salary rule are make-or-break. The **salary relative to thresholds matters most**, since failing to meet it means automatic refusal. Unique rules: recognized sponsors expedite processing, and specialized tracks exist (e.g. *orientation year* graduates get the reduced salary).

### Orientation Year (Zoekjaar) Permit

This one-year **job-seeker/work permit** allows recent graduates and researchers to stay and work freely. It requires that the applicant finished a study or PhD program **within the last 3 years**[\[35\]](https://business.gov.nl/coming-to-the-netherlands/permits-and-visa/residence-permit-for-orientation-year/#:~:text=You%20can%20apply%20for%20the,if%20they%20meet%20certain%20conditions). If the degree is from a Dutch or top-ranked foreign institution, the applicant is eligible. (Foreign degrees must be from a university in the world’s top-200 by major rankings[\[36\]](https://business.gov.nl/coming-to-the-netherlands/permits-and-visa/residence-permit-for-orientation-year/#:~:text=If%20your%20study%20programme%20was,same%20publisher%20count%20as%20one).) There are no salary or employer requirements – in fact, **no sponsorship** is needed. The holder may work any job or start a business without a separate work permit[\[37\]](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/residence-permit-for-orientation-year#:~:text=You%20may%20work%20freely%20in,freely%20permitted%2C%20TWV%20not%20required%E2%80%99). Documents needed: degree certificate, immigration forms, and proof of grad date. This permit has **no points**, just pass/fail eligibility. The crucial criteria are recency and accreditation of the degree. Unique: it cannot be extended beyond 1 year[\[38\]](https://business.gov.nl/coming-to-the-netherlands/permits-and-visa/residence-permit-for-orientation-year/#:~:text=How%20long%20is%20the%20permit,valid), but many use it as a bridge to get experience or switch to the Highly Skilled Migrant permit (with a lower salary threshold)[\[39\]](https://business.gov.nl/coming-to-the-netherlands/permits-and-visa/residence-permit-for-orientation-year/#:~:text=Do%20you%20find%20a%20job,a%20lower%20income%20requirement%20applies).

## Germany

### EU Blue Card

Germany’s **EU Blue Card** is for high-qualification specialists. Criteria include holding a university degree (or equivalent vocational qualification) and a **job offer of ≥6 months**[\[40\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=,also%20obtain%20an%20EU%20Blue). The salary must meet the threshold: for 2025, **€48,300 gross per year** for general occupations, or **€43,760** for shortage occupations (STEM, medicine, academia, etc., as listed by the Federal Employment Agency)[\[41\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=or%20in%20prospect%20at%20the,be%20%20145%20in%20Germany). For recent graduates (within 3 years of degree), the lower threshold €43,760 also applies[\[42\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=EU%20Blue%20Card%20for%20young,professionals). The job must match the applicant’s qualifications; if it is a regulated profession (doctor, pharmacist, etc.), the license must be in hand or imminent[\[43\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=,also%20obtain%20an%20EU%20Blue). Required documents: completed Blue Card application, passport, university diploma (with German recognition), employer’s job contract detailing salary and duties, and health insurance. Employers often assist with paperwork. No quota or cap applies to Blue Cards in Germany. The decision is rule-based: salary and degree are **decisive criteria**; failure on either leads to rejection. Unique rules: the permit is issued for the contract length (up to 4 years), after 21–33 months holders may apply for settlement (depending on language proficiency)[\[44\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=The%20EU%20Blue%20Card%20is,if%20certain%20conditions%20are%20met), and family reunification has minimal requirements (no German needed)[\[30\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Family%20reunification%20is%20based%20on,also%20be%20made%20in%20Germany).

### Intra-Corporate Transfer (ICT Card)

Germany follows the EU ICT Directive with a special **ICT residence permit** for managers, specialists or trainees transferred by their non-EU company to a German branch[\[45\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=You%20can%20enter%2C%20reside%20and,Corporate%20Transferee%20permit). Key conditions: the transferee must have been employed by the sending company for **at least 6 months**[\[46\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Please%20note%20that%20before%20being,months%3B%20a%20trainee%206%20months), and the assignment in Germany must exceed **90 days**[\[47\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Further%20requirements%20The%20intra,Duration%20of%20validity%20of%20permits). No minimum salary is set by law, but the transfer package must be “appropriate.” Required documents include the (signed) transfer contract or work contract covering the period, an assignment letter detailing duties, remuneration and conditions[\[48\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Document%20that%20shows%20that%20the,of%20the%20Federal%20Employment%20Agency), and proof of the employee’s qualifications. Approval by the Federal Employment Agency is generally not needed under the ICT rules. The permit can be issued for up to 3 years (managers/specialists) or 1 year (trainees)[\[49\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=The%20period%20of%20validity%20of,1%20year%20for%20trainee%20employees). The **central criterion** is the genuine intracompany relationship and purpose of transfer; salary and seniority are subsidiary (though a realistic compensation is expected). Unique: ICT holders benefit from EU mobility rules (up to 90-day moves within other EU branches)[\[50\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Mobility), and family reunification follows Blue Card rules (no language requirement)[\[30\]](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/eu-immigration-portal/intra-corporate-transferee-ict-germany_en#:~:text=Family%20reunification%20is%20based%20on,also%20be%20made%20in%20Germany).

## United States

### O-1A (Extraordinary Ability in Sciences/Business/Athletics)

The **O-1A visa** is for individuals with “extraordinary ability” in fields like science, business, education or athletics. The applicant must show sustained national or international acclaim in their field. There is **no salary threshold**, but evidence of achievement is required. USCIS lists criteria such as major awards (Nobel Prize, Olympic medal), membership in exclusive organizations, published material about the person, original contributions of major significance, authorship of scholarly articles, high salary relative to peers, etc. (The applicant must meet 3 out of 6 standard criteria, e.g. membership, awards, publications)[\[51\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/o-1-visa-explained#:~:text=The%20forms%20of%20acceptable%20evidence,submit%203%20of%20the%20following). Key documents: Form I-129 filed by a U.S. employer or agent, an advisory opinion from a peer group (e.g. union or industry experts), the job contract (showing duties in the field of expertise), and extensive evidence of the beneficiary’s achievements (awards certificates, press clippings, letters of recommendation). The **advisory opinion and quality of evidence matter most**—USCIS looks for credible endorsements that the person is truly at the top of their profession[\[52\]](https://www.pathlawgroup.com/o1b-visa-requirements/#:~:text=For%20all%20other%20candidates%2C%20at,qualify%20for%20the%20O1B%20visa)[\[51\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/o-1-visa-explained#:~:text=The%20forms%20of%20acceptable%20evidence,submit%203%20of%20the%20following). Unique: the O-1 allows dual intent (can pursue a green card), and employers or agents petition on behalf of the applicant (a U.S. petitioner is required).

### O-1B (Extraordinary Ability in Arts/Entertainment)

The **O-1B visa** is similar, but for artists and entertainers. Applicants must demonstrate extraordinary achievements in their artistic field (cinema, music, fashion, etc.). Qualifying evidence includes major international awards (Oscars, Cannes prize, etc.), leading roles in distinguished productions, critical reviews, high earnings compared to peers, and other accolades[\[52\]](https://www.pathlawgroup.com/o1b-visa-requirements/#:~:text=For%20all%20other%20candidates%2C%20at,qualify%20for%20the%20O1B%20visa)[\[53\]](https://www.pathlawgroup.com/o1b-visa-requirements/#:~:text=in%20the%20performing%20arts%2C%20as,in%20the%20motion%20picture%20industry). The petitioner (employer or agent) files Form I-129 with supporting documents: the artist’s portfolio of awards, press, contracts for U.S. performances, and a peer advisory letter from a relevant industry group. The evaluation is qualitative and **case-by-case**; there is no fixed score or salary. The **most important factors** are proof of recognized prominence (e.g. awards) and a valid U.S. job offer in the same artistic field[\[52\]](https://www.pathlawgroup.com/o1b-visa-requirements/#:~:text=For%20all%20other%20candidates%2C%20at,qualify%20for%20the%20O1B%20visa).

### H-1B (Specialty Occupation Worker)

The **H-1B visa** is for professional-level workers in “specialty occupations,” typically requiring a bachelor’s degree. Key criteria: the job must require a minimum of a bachelor’s degree (or equivalent) in a specific field[\[54\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/the-h-1b-visa-explained#:~:text=H)[\[55\]](https://www.visum-usa.com/en/us-visa-categories/h-1b-visa.html#:~:text=The%20basic%20requirement%20for%20the,university%20degree%20in%20their%20field), and the applicant must have such a degree or equivalent experience. The U.S. employer **sponsors** the visa by filing a Labor Condition Application (LCA) with the Department of Labor (attesting to paying the prevailing wage and not harming U.S. workers)[\[56\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/the-h-1b-visa-explained#:~:text=2,job%20role%20at%20the%20worksite)[\[57\]](https://www.visum-usa.com/en/us-visa-categories/h-1b-visa.html#:~:text=In%20this%20context%2C%20the%20offered,applicant%20is%20to%20be%20employed). There is an annual cap (65,000 plus 20,000 for U.S. Master’s holders) which triggers a lottery if demand exceeds supply[\[58\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/the-h-1b-visa-explained#:~:text=meet%20specific%20criteria%3A). Required documents include the degree diploma (and foreign degree evaluation if needed), the job offer letter, the certified LCA, and the petition (Form I-129) with all supporting evidence. The system is largely rule-based: applicant must **have the degree** and job must **meet specialty criteria**[\[54\]](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/the-h-1b-visa-explained#:~:text=H)[\[55\]](https://www.visum-usa.com/en/us-visa-categories/h-1b-visa.html#:~:text=The%20basic%20requirement%20for%20the,university%20degree%20in%20their%20field). Salary must be **at least the prevailing wage** for the occupation at that location[\[57\]](https://www.visum-usa.com/en/us-visa-categories/h-1b-visa.html#:~:text=In%20this%20context%2C%20the%20offered,applicant%20is%20to%20be%20employed). The **degree match and LCA compliance matter most**. Unique features: H-1B has a strict quota and lottery, employment is employer-specific, and the visa is initially granted for 3 years (renewable once, generally). Dual intent is allowed (beneficiaries can apply for permanent residence).

## Common Patterns and Differences

Across these countries, **common patterns** include:  
\- A **formal job offer or contract** from a qualifying employer or organization is required in almost every case. Immigration authorities examine the nature of the job and employer carefully (e.g. registered sponsor or corporate group)[\[31\]](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant#:~:text=,And%20do%20you%20practise%20a)[\[12\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-a/#:~:text=1,travel%20document%20of%20the%20foreigner).  
\- **Education and/or experience** thresholds are ubiquitous. Most visas demand a relevant degree or substantial professional background (Ireland, France, NL, DE, US H-1B all require at least bachelor/Master’s level). The exact requirement varies (e.g. O-1 focuses on achievements rather than formal credentials).  
\- **Salary or wage criteria** feature in many systems. Several countries set a minimum gross salary (Ireland, Netherlands, Germany, France, US H-1B’s prevailing wage). The thresholds may be absolute amounts or multipliers (France uses a multiple of minimum wage[\[23\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=,time%20worker), Germany a fixed amount by year[\[41\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=or%20in%20prospect%20at%20the,be%20%20145%20in%20Germany), Netherlands uses age-based monthly rates[\[34\]](https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements#application-to-work-as-a-highly-skilled-migrant-and-for-the-european-blue-card#:~:text=Highly%20skilled%20migrants%20reduced%20salary,00)).  
\- **Employer rules**: Authorized sponsorship is essential. Employers often must be recognized or registered (NL sponsor list[\[31\]](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant#:~:text=,And%20do%20you%20practise%20a), Ireland’s Employer Registration Number, French company affiliation, etc.). Employers may need to post bonds or prove financial capacity (Poland B permit requires showing company income[\[16\]](https://cudzoziemiec.bydgoszcz.pl/en/work/work-permit-type-b/#:~:text=5,statement%20of%20no%20criminal%20conviction)).  
\- **Labor market considerations**: Some visas require a labor-market test (Ireland’s General permit, Poland Type A) while others waive it for highly skilled categories (Ireland Critical Skills, France Talent, Blue Card).  
\- **Point systems** are generally *absent*. Most schemes are rule-based; applicants either meet criteria or not. The result is a binary pass/fail on each key requirement. (An exception is point-based systems like Canada/Australia, but those are not asked here.)

**Significant differences** include:  
\- **Purpose and scope**: Some visas target high-end talent (EU Blue Card, Critical Skills, O-1) versus more general skilled workers (Ireland General, H-1B, Poland A) or even students (NL Orientation Year). The O-1 is unique in focusing on *individual acclaim* rather than meeting an occupational definition.  
\- **Family provisions**: Talent-focused permits often grant family open work rights (Ireland Critical Skills, France Talent, EU Blue Card, US O-1)[\[3\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/#:~:text=,currently%20issued%20free%20of%20charge)[\[27\]](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/france-visas/international-talents-and-economic-attractiveness#:~:text=Your%20family%20members%20may%20also,your%20local%20police%20station%20in)[\[44\]](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card#:~:text=The%20EU%20Blue%20Card%20is,if%20certain%20conditions%20are%20met), whereas others may restrict dependents (Ireland General does not allow spouse work[\[7\]](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/general-employment-permit/#:~:text=Spouses%2C%20dependants%20or%20partners%20of,permit%20in%20their%20own%20right), H-1B spouses (H-4) have limited rights).  
\- **Renewal and permanency paths**: Some visas lead quickly to permanent residency (e.g. EU Blue Card has fast-track, Ireland Critical Skills encourages long-term residency), whereas others are time-bound (NL Orientation Year is non-extendable) or have unique transitions (US H-1B extends but is annual-capped).  
\- **Labor market integration**: Countries differ on requiring labor tests. Ireland and Poland emphasize protecting local workers with tests or quotas, whereas EU Blue Card and French Talent remove this hurdle for skilled categories.  
\- **Salary benchmarks**: The level and structure vary widely. Germany and Netherlands publish exact figures (indexed annually), France ties to minimum wage multiples, and the US uses a complex “prevailing wage” rule. Poland’s work permits do *not* specify salary minima (except that employment must be on payroll).  
\- **Sponsorship of employer vs. self**: In most cases an employer must petition (Ireland, NL, DE, US H-1B/O-1). The US O-1 can be petitioned by an agent. Poland’s permits are strictly employer-sponsored. Netherlands Orientation Year and France Talent Investor categories are exceptions allowing more independent applicants.

Overall, **education/skill level, valid job offers, and adequate compensation** form the backbone of evaluation in all systems. Variations arise in how these are quantified (lists of eligible occupations, minimum wages, experience tests) and in procedural rules (caps, employer registrations, work tests).

## Generalized Rule-Based Scoring Framework

One can abstract a **unified visa scoring model** on a 0–100 scale by mapping key criteria to numeric weights. For example:

score \= 0  
if has\_required\_degree\_or\_equivalent: score \+= 20  
if work\_experience\_in\_field \>= threshold: score \+= 10  
if job\_offer\_exists\_with\_minimum\_duration: score \+= 15  
if salary \>= country\_minimum\_for\_role: score \+= 20  
if employer\_is\_authorized\_sponsor: score \+= 10  
if occupation\_on\_shortage\_list: score \+= 5  
if labor\_market\_test\_passed\_or\_not\_required: score \+= 10  
if critical\_other (licenses, language, etc): score \+= 10

In JSON-like rule logic, an evaluation might look like:

{  
  "education": { "hasDegree": true, "points": 20 },  
  "experience": { "years": 5, "threshold": 3, "points": 10 },  
  "jobOffer": { "durationMonths": 12, "minMonths": 6, "points": 15 },  
  "salary": { "offered": 60000, "threshold": 50000, "points": 20 },  
  "employer": { "isRecognized": true, "points": 10 },  
  "shortageOccupation": { "isShortage": false, "points": 0 },  
  "laborTest": { "passed": true, "points": 10 },  
  "others": { "license": true, "language": false, "points": 5 }  
}

Each rule element yields a sub-score; the sum can be normalized to 0–100. For example, meeting all criteria might score 90+. If a key criterion is missing (e.g. salary too low), its points drop to 0 and greatly reduce the total score. This framework can be tailored: e.g. salary could be a sliding scale, giving partial points if closer to the threshold. Pseudocode example:

def evaluate\_applicant(applicant, visa\_rules):  
    score \= 0  
    \# Education  
    if applicant.degree \== visa\_rules.required\_degree:  
        score \+= 20  
    elif applicant.experience\_years \>= visa\_rules.degree\_equivalent\_experience:  
        score \+= 15  
    \# Salary  
    if applicant.salary \>= visa\_rules.salary\_threshold:  
        score \+= 20  
    elif applicant.salary \>= 0.9 \* visa\_rules.salary\_threshold:  
        score \+= 10  
    \# ...  
    return min(score, 100\)

This rule-based scoring aligns with how visa officers apply cutoffs: each mandatory criterion either contributes full points or zero. The framework can output a JSON report of which rules passed or failed, aiding transparency.

## AI-Driven Personalization

Integrating an AI (e.g. OpenAI’s API) can add *qualitative insight* on visa cases. A data-driven engine could:

* **Analyze individual profiles:** Given applicant data (CV, job offer, documents), use an LLM to **summarize strengths/weaknesses**. For example, “You have a Master’s degree and 5 years’ experience, which meets the education criteria. Your job’s salary is slightly below the threshold for a Blue Card, which could be an issue. Strengths: qualified occupation, recognized employer; Improvement: negotiate a higher salary or show additional achievements.” This personalized advice can explain the rule-based score.

* **Suggest improvements:** By querying the AI with scenario data, it can propose actionable suggestions. For instance, “Your current H-1B case failed the lottery; the AI might suggest focusing on cap-exempt employment or applying next cycle with premium processing.” It can generate rationales like a human immigration consultant, customizing to the candidate’s profile.

* **Guidance on docs:** The AI can list **missing documents** or alternative evidence. E.g. if a question asks “What extra evidence do I need?”, the AI might analyze the rule framework and say: “You lacked membership in a professional organization (O-1 criterion). Consider joining the XYZ Society to bolster your profile.”

* **Rule-checking:** The LLM can codify border cases. For example, “Is an MBA from a foreign unaccredited institution acceptable for H-1B?” The model can check the rule (“evaluation needed”) and advise seeking a credential evaluation or additional experience.

Technically, an application might use a pipeline: collect structured data on an applicant, compute a preliminary score via the rule engine, then prompt GPT-4 with the profile and visa criteria. The AI returns a natural-language assessment (citing, if needed, the relevant rules) and recommendations.
